I don't like Anthropic anymore

The screen you hit when you run out used to tell you when to come back. Now it tells you how to pay.

NinjaHawk July 23, 2026

I've had a Max subscription for about a year. I use Claude Code, which comes with it. I like the models and I'm not here to complain about the models. This is about one screen. The one you hit when you've used up what the plan gives you for the week, and what that screen started asking me for.

For most of the year it told you a time. You've hit your limit, it comes back at some hour, go do something else. I hit it plenty. It was annoying and it was honest. I paid for a bucket, the bucket was empty, and it would fill back up on a schedule I could plan around.

Now the same wall leads with a button. What I get, more or less word for word, is "You've reached your usage limit. Turn on usage credits to keep going." The reset time is still in there if you go looking for it. It just isn't the message anymore. The message is that there's a way to keep paying, and it shows up at the one moment you're stuck on something and want to keep going.

That's a small change and it sounds like nothing. It bothered me more the longer I sat with it, so I went and read how the thing it's pointing at actually works.

What usage credits are

This part isn't a rumor. It's in Anthropic's own help center. Once you pass your plan's limit, usage credits let you keep working, billed at standard API rates, charged on their own line separately from the subscription you already pay. You switch them on under Settings > Usage, and the setup runs like this:

StepWhat it does
EnableTurns on pay-as-you-go past your plan.
Adjust limitSet a monthly cap, or choose "Set to unlimited" for no spending restrictions.
Add fundsPrepay a balance for it to draw down.
Auto-reloadBuys more on its own when the balance drops below a number you pick. Daily redemption limit: $2,000.

The one that stops me is the cap. You can put a ceiling on the month, or you can pick "Set to unlimited," which is their phrasing, and it does what it sounds like. So the same page that warns you you're running low is also the page offering, a click away, to take the ceiling off. The article signs off with the line these pages always end on: "Price and plans are subject to change at Anthropic's discretion."

Selling overflow by the token isn't the problem. Plenty of things work that way and nobody writes a blog post about their electric bill. What changed is what the subscription is for. I bought it as a cap. A fixed number I'd hand over each month and never have to think about again. The product has quietly started treating that number as a floor, and the screen where I used to just wait is now the screen where the spending starts.

The toggle that doesn't stay put

What actually pushed me from annoyed to done was reading that the switch doesn't always stay where you leave it. There's a bug report on the Claude Code repo, issue #25647, filed after an update earlier this year. The opening line:

The "Extra usage" option on claude.ai was automatically enabled without my explicit consent, resulting in charges exceeding my configured $50 monthly spending limit. I was charged $74.99 (150% of the limit), leaving a negative balance of -$15.92.

They'd turned it off. An update turned it back on with no notice, and by the time they noticed they were already sitting at a negative balance, charged half again over the fifty-dollar cap they had set specifically so this couldn't happen. The report got closed as invalid, with the note "Issue doesn't seem to be related to Claude Code," because the billing lives on the website and not in the command-line tool. That's technically correct and it's also the part that sticks with me. The charge belongs to a corner of the product that the person hitting it has no clean way to reach.

I want to be careful here, because this is the point where a post like this usually reaches for more than it has. This is one report I can link to, plus the ordinary scatter of people on forums saying some version of the same thing happened to them. I haven't reproduced it and I'm not going to tell you it's happening to everybody. What I'll actually stand behind is narrower. Every piece you'd need for it to happen is real and already shipped: a toggle that can turn on, an unlimited setting sitting right next to it, and an auto-reload that buys more without asking again. Whether it flips itself on, I can't prove. That it could is just how the page is built.

What this means

The part I'm sure of is small and documented. The limit message went from telling you a time to offering you a purchase. Usage credits are real, they bill at API rates on top of the plan, there's an unlimited option, and there's auto-reload. At least one person got charged past a cap they set after the switch came back on by itself. All of that is linked above and none of it is my interpretation.

What I think it means is that the subscription stopped being the ceiling. The moment you put a "keep going" button on the wall, the wall turns into the place the next sale starts, and they've placed it in front of you at the exact moment you're mid-task and least inclined to stop and think about it. That's not an accident of the UI. Somebody chose to lead with the button.

What I don't know is whether this is a growth team trying out a button or the plan being slowly turned into the front door for metered billing. Could be either. My guess is it drifts toward the second, because that's where the money is and nothing in the design is pushing the other way. I can't see their reasons. I can see the incentive, and it only points in one direction.

I still pay them. I'll probably pay them again next month, because the alternatives are worse and the work is real. But I read the billing page every week now, and a year ago it never once occurred to me that I'd need to. That's the whole change. It's small, and it turned out to be enough.
